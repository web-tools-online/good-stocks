Stock screener: US, EU & Czech stocks

Seven fundamental checks on every listed company, from revenue growth to PEG. The data refreshes every week.

Holdings: 0

A stock passes a criterion when its value meets the threshold. Missing data counts as a fail, except PEG: a stock without analysts' growth forecasts is scored without it (e.g. 7/7). The acquirer's multiple, the F-score, the standard deviation channel and the channel trend are switched off by default. Untick a criterion to ignore it. Your settings are saved in this browser.

How it works

Coverage

US: every common stock and ADR on NASDAQ, NYSE, NYSE American, NYSE Arca and Cboe, from the Nasdaq Trader symbol directory.

EU: stocks of EU-based companies on the main exchange of each EU country that Yahoo Finance covers: Xetra, Euronext (Paris, Amsterdam, Brussels, Lisbon, Dublin), Borsa Italiana, Madrid, Vienna, Nasdaq Nordic & Baltic, Warsaw, Athens, Budapest and Bucharest. A company listed on several of them appears once, on its home exchange.

Czech: all shares traded on the Prague Stock Exchange.

Metrics

Revenue Growth (1Y, TTM)
Revenue over the last 12 months compared with the 12 months before. * marks an estimate: the latest quarter against the same quarter a year earlier.
Earnings / Revenue Growth (4Y)
Change in net income or revenue across the last four fiscal years: the latest year compared with the oldest of the four (e.g. FY2022 → FY2025).
ROE
Net income (TTM) ÷ shareholders' equity.
Debt to Equity
Total debt ÷ shareholders' equity (most recent quarter).
Free Cash Flow (TTM)
Operating cash flow − capital expenditure, last 12 months.
FCF Yield
Free cash flow (TTM) ÷ market cap. Checks the price without relying on forecasts. The S&P 500 as a whole yields about 3 %, so the default of 4 % means cheaper than the market.
Dividend Yield
Yahoo Finance's annual dividend ÷ share price: the expected dividend, or the last 12 months' dividends when there is no forecast. A yield of 15 % or more is checked against the dividends the company actually paid. If it is more than twice that amount, the paid amount is shown instead, marked *.
PEG (5Y expected)
P/E ÷ analysts' expected yearly earnings growth over the next 5 years. Many European companies have no analyst forecast; they are scored without PEG (e.g. 7/7 instead of x/8).
Acquirer's Multiple
Enterprise value ÷ operating income (TTM), as used by Tobias Carlisle. Enterprise value = market cap + debt + preferred stock + minority interest − cash and short-term investments. It shows what a buyer of the whole company pays for its operating earnings; lower is cheaper. Not shown when operating income or enterprise value is not positive (it means little for banks and insurers).
F-Score
Joseph Piotroski's 0–9 score of financial health, comparing the latest fiscal year with the one before. One point each for: positive return on assets, positive operating cash flow, a higher return on assets, operating cash flow above net income, lower long-term debt relative to assets, a higher current ratio, no new shares issued, a higher gross margin and a higher asset turnover. 8–9 is strong, 0–2 weak. Hover a value to see which tests passed. Shown only when all nine tests can be evaluated.
Std Dev Channel
Where today's price sits in a linear regression channel of the last 2,500 daily closes (about ten years), the same as TradingView's standard deviation / linear regression channel with Length 2500 and Source Close. A straight line is fitted through the prices; the value says how many standard deviations of the prices around that line the current price is above (+) or below (−) it. −1σ means on the lower −1 line, −2σ on the −2 line. The criterion (off by default) looks for prices at or below a chosen line, i.e. cheap relative to the stock's own long-term trend. Hover a value for the price of each line and the trend of the line per year; the Channel Trend column shows whether the line rises or falls. Listings with less than two years of prices have no value.
Channel Trend
How fast the regression line of the standard deviation channel rises (+) or falls (−) per year, as a percentage of its current value. A negative trend means the stock has been in a long downtrend, so a low channel position may not mean it is cheap. The criterion (off by default) trend ≥ 0 %/yr, combined with the Std Dev Channel criterion, finds stocks that are low within a rising long-term trend.

Data & updates

A GitHub Actions workflow runs every Saturday. It collects the ticker lists, pulls the current fundamentals from Yahoo Finance and stores them in a SQLite database. Each run replaces the previous week's numbers. This page is then rebuilt from the database.

Yahoo's per-share figures (dividend per share, earnings per share) are occasionally broken, usually right after a reverse split. Implausible values are checked against the company's totals and hidden if those don't back them up: a dividend larger than the share price, a 15 %+ yield the company never paid, a P/E below 1. Ordinary values are shown as Yahoo reports them.

You can download the database and query it with any SQLite tool.

For information only, not investment advice. The data can be incomplete or wrong; always check a company's own filings.
